NORDIC CLIFFSIDE RETREAT

Wedge-Shaped Timber Cabin · Biophilic Integration · Cliff-Edge Structural Anchor

  • LocationPreikestolen, Lysefjorden, Norway
  • ProgrammeBachelor of Architecture — Design Studio
  • Scope100 sqm Holiday Cabin, Structural Point Anchor, Avian Habitat Integration, Sectional Stacking
  • ToolsRhino 3D, AutoCAD, Adobe Photoshop, V-Ray, DesignBuilder

A 100 sqm cliff-edge timber shelter perched above Lysefjorden.

Designed around the dramatic precipice of Preikestolen, the project reconciles extreme topography, dense surrounding forest, human shelter, and native bird-nesting habitats. The resulting wedge-shaped volume anchors to a single primary point on the rock, with its angled roof plane framing fjord views while leaving the rock face beneath undisturbed.

01. Site & Form Concept

Balancing human and avian cliff habitats. The concept diagram frames the rock precipice, pine forest, human residence, and nesting fauna as equal design parameters, generating a sloped profile that acts as a natural extension of the mountain ridge.

02. Design Process & Structural Stacking

Three-pass assembly logic. A pin-connected steel substructure anchors the volume into bedrock, internal vertical ladders establish circulation through the frame, and an insulated timber shell wraps the exterior to integrate the cabin with the surrounding forest.

The continuous triangular envelope houses three distinct vertical levels: an attic observation nook under the glazed roof plane, a middle sleeping platform, and a compact bathroom and service core at the base.

03. Floor Plans & Modulated Grid

1 / 3

All three floors are aligned to a 2460 / 1930 / 740 mm structural bay grid, allowing the vertical ladder core to pass cleanly from the lowest bedrock entry to the top attic ridge.

04. Interior Atmosphere & Biophilic Spatial Views

1 / 3

Slotted timber fins filter zenithal natural light while allowing living landscape features—including existing tree trunks—to pass directly through the floor plates.

05. Academic & Design Takeaways

  • 1. Topography-Driven Profile Deriving the triangular cross-section directly from the cliff angle allowed the building to act as a natural ridge extension rather than an imposed mass.
  • 2. Non-Human Centric Programming Incorporating nesting fauna into the massing parameters resulted in an elevated deck profile that protects rock-face ecosystems below.
  • 3. Vertical Micro-Zoning Stacking an attic observatory, sleeping lounge, and compact service base within one triangular envelope maximized usability across a 100 sqm footprint.
  • 4. Biophilic Structural Integration Preserving existing site trees by passing them directly through the living spaces reinforced a direct physical dialogue between interior spaces and the surrounding forest.
