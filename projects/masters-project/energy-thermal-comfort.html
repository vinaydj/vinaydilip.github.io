ENERGY USE & THERMAL COMFORT SIMULATION

Parametric Single-Zone Building Performance Simulation — AEBN05
Evaluating the influence of solar orientation and surrounding urban shading context on Energy Use Intensity and zone operative temperature
  • Location Lund University, LTH — Faculty of Engineering
  • Course AEBN05 — Energy Use & Thermal Comfort Simulation
  • Term Autumn 2024
  • Author Vinay Dilip Jayaselan — Individual work
PDF Full Report

How sensitive is early-stage building performance to basic massing and contextual decisions? This project developed a parametric single-zone model in Rhino and Grasshopper, simulated through ClimateStudio using Copenhagen climate data, to isolate how solar orientation and external contextual shading dictate Energy Use Intensity (EUI) and operative temperature swings.

OVERVIEW & METHODOLOGY

The investigation modeled a representative 4.5 × 4.5 m single-zone space (3 m floor-to-ceiling height) with a south-facing aperture[cite: 12]. Baseline envelope parameters were configured with a uniform U-value of 0.2 W/m²K, a heating setpoint of 20°C, and occupancy/lighting internal gain profiles[cite: 12]. The parametric definition allowed rapid permutation testing across cardinal rotations (90°, 180°, 270°) and contextual massing obstructions[cite: 12].

THERMAL COMFORT & ORIENTATION ANALYSIS

1 / 7

Zone operative temperature remains stable near 19–20°C throughout winter heating schedules, but escalates significantly during unconditioned summer peaks[cite: 12]. Rotating the primary aperture away from the south orientation removes beneficial passive winter solar gains, causing annual heating demand and EUI to nearly double[cite: 12].

TAKEAWAYS

  • Orientation governs baseline energy demand. Rotating the single-zone room away from its optimal south-facing orientation increased EUI from 45.44 to 88.51 kWh/m², demonstrating that solar geometry outweighs minor envelope adjustments[cite: 12].
  • External obstruction mirrors orientation loss. Introducing an adjacent contextual building reduced peak solar overheating but concurrently eliminated winter passive heat gains, matching the EUI penalty of unfavorable rotations[cite: 12].
  • Passive cooling controls are necessary to manage summer peaks. Without active cooling or dynamic exterior shading, high window-to-wall ratios cause operative temperatures to spike well beyond 30°C in unconditioned periods[cite: 12].
  • Parametric modeling accelerates sensitivity benchmarking. Constructing the simulation pipeline in Grasshopper and ClimateStudio allowed immediate multi-variable comparisons without rebuilding spatial assets[cite: 12].
