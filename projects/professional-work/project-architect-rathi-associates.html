Balancing high-density commercial real estate with climate-responsive design and statutory code compliance.

During my tenure at Rathi Associates, I spearheaded the design development, environmental analysis, and authority sanction documentation for high-density commercial developments. My core portfolio focused on contrasting typologies: Rohan Tech (2B+G+11), an urban infill high-rise, and NMR-50P (2B+G+8), a terraced suburban SEZ campus tower.

01. Rohan Tech — Commercial Office Tower

Rohan Tech (2B+G+11) is an urban commercial office tower optimized for maximum usable floor area on a constrained infill site. The design study details comprehensive statutory compliance, including NBC fire egress travel distance limits, dual-staircase compartmentalization, shaft ventilation sizing, and vehicular turning radii. The glazed façade integrates high-performance low-E glass with deep horizontal mullions to reduce solar heat gain on exposed west and south orientations.

02. NMR-50P — SEZ Commercial Campus

NMR-50P (2B+G+8) is a terraced corporate office campus located in an airport Special Economic Zone (SEZ). The building volume steps back at the upper floors to introduce open-air landscaped terrace gardens. Six distributed vertical cores ensure strict fire travel compliance across wide floorplates, while an integrated peripheral bus bay, drop-off concourse, and water retention body manage heavy transit flows and stormwater run-off.

06. Professional Takeaways

  • 1. Typology-Specific Egress Design Designing compact urban infill towers (Rohan Tech) versus sprawling suburban SEZ campuses (NMR-50P) required distinct vertical core distribution strategies to strictly satisfy NBC fire travel distance and staircase pressurization standards.
  • 2. Climate-Responsive Façade Engineering Calibrating Window-to-Wall Ratios (WWR), horizontal louvers, and spandrel depths according to solar orientation reduced peak solar heat gain on west-facing elevations while optimizing daylight penetration for office perimeters.
  • 3. Double-Basement Infrastructure Coordination Coordinating deep structural transfer grids with MEP plant rooms, pump rooms, and parking ramps in dual-basement layouts prevented critical spatial clashes prior to authority sanction submissions.
  • 4. Multi-Sector Execution & GFC Delivery Managing comprehensive architectural sets—from high-density corporate towers and private bespoke residences to large-scale plotted development master plans—demanded high-precision drafting and cross-disciplinary alignment.
