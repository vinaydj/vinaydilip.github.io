01. Central University Campus (CUAP)

Master plan, spatial zoning, and Detailed Project Report (DPR) for the Central University of Andhra Pradesh on a 491.3-acre site at Janthaluru, Ananthapuramu. Designed to accommodate 4,508 students across 5 schools and 11 departments, the master plan integrates academic, residential, healthcare, sports, and civic infrastructure. The campus layout is organized around a continuous central green spine that buffers academic zones from vehicular circulation, reserving 27% of the site for future institutional expansion while prioritizing complete pedestrian and cycle connectivity.

The institutional buildings follow a modular architectural language inspired by classical colonnades. Shaded internal courtyards and covered walkways protect pedestrians from direct solar heat gain, while the distinctive circular red-brick library acts as the central architectural landmark of the university.

02. Sustainability & Environmental Systems

GRIHA-aligned sustainability framework. Environmental strategies include canal-top photovoltaic arrays, deep groundwater recharge wells, earth-air tunnels for passive pre-cooling, occupancy-sensored daylight integration, and dense native afforestation belts acting as dust and thermal buffers across the campus perimeter.

04. Design & Planning Takeaways

  • 1. Macro-Scale Spatial Hierarchy Master planning across a 491.3-acre site demonstrated that buffer zones, microclimate corridors, and strict pedestrian-vehicular separation are just as vital to campus functionality as individual building architecture.
  • 2. Institutional Cohesion Through Repetition Carrying a modular, historically referenced colonnade language across administrative and academic blocks established a unified institutional identity across distinct building programs.
  • 3. Distinct Landmark Anchors Deliberately breaking the rectilinear grid with a circular red-brick library provided an essential orientation anchor across the broader central green spine.
  • 4. Healthcare & Institutional Phasing Reserving 27% of land for future academic expansion and segregating complex clinical flows in the Chikkaballapura Medical Institute ensured that long-term institutional growth could occur without disrupting operational circulation.
