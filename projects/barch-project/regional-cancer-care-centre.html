REGIONAL CANCER CARE CENTRE

Comprehensive Oncology & Research Hospital — B.Arch Thesis
Evidence-based oncology healthcare infrastructure combining biophilic transition spaces, radioactive containment zoning, and structural cantilevered recovery wards
  • Location Cochin Medical College Campus, Kalamassery, Kochi, Kerala
  • Typology Comprehensive Oncology & Research Hospital
  • Scope Out-Patient & Diagnostic Block, In-Patient & Emergency Tower, Healing Landscape
  • Tools Autodesk Revit, AutoCAD, SketchUp, Lumion, Photoshop
PDF Thesis Report

Addressing the regional healthcare deficit through evidence-based oncology architecture. Kerala's high cancer incidence created an acute demand for a dedicated regional comprehensive cancer center in central Kerala. Sited alongside the Cochin Cancer Research Centre in Kalamassery, this thesis develops a full clinical care campus structured around evidence-based healing principles, biophilic transition spaces, and clear clinical zoning.

The campus is bifurcated into two functionally independent yet linked volumes: an Out-Patient & Diagnostic Block handling high-volume consultations, radiology, and day-care chemotherapy, and an elevated In-Patient & Emergency Tower holding sterile surgical suites, ICUs, and private recovery wards floating above a landscaped reflecting pool.

01. FEASIBILITY & SITE ANALYSIS

Macro-level mapping situated the site within a public/semi-public health institutional buffer zone. Microclimate studies guided building orientation along the east-west axis to minimize direct solar thermal gain on ward facades while capturing prevailing south-westerly monsoon breezes across central courtyards.

02. BENCHMARK PRECEDENTS & CLINICAL RESEARCH

1 / 6

Four international hospital case studies informed department sizing, staff-to-bed ratios, and chemotherapy suite orientation. Clinical research established strict septic/aseptic zoning boundaries and demonstrated the psychological benefits of natural daylight and green landscape exposure in oncology patient recovery.

03. MASTER PLAN & TRANSITION ARCHITECTURE

The master plan organizes fifteen vehicular access, patient drop-off, and service logistics points. Shaded colonnades, open pergolas, and landscaped water retention bodies act as psychological buffers between bustling public outpatient zones and quiet, focused recovery wards.

04. OUT-PATIENT & DIAGNOSTIC BLOCK

1 / 5

The basement anchors heavy radio-shielded infrastructure, including two Linear Accelerator (LINAC) vaults, cyclotron isotope production, and MRI/CT suites accessed via a dedicated service ramp. The ground level features a radial open food court and registration atrium, while upper floors accommodate day-care chemotherapy suites with direct views of the therapeutic gardens.

05. IN-PATIENT & EMERGENCY TOWER

1 / 5

Inspired by the structural language of Lina Bo Bardi's MASP, the in-patient tower utilizes post-tensioned concrete shear walls and cantilevered piers to elevate patient recovery wards above an open ground plane. Clinical acuity follows a vertical gradient: emergency trauma at the ground base, sterile surgical suites and ICUs on the first level, and peaceful inpatient rooms on the upper floors.

06. 3D SPATIAL PERSPECTIVES

1 / 7

TAKEAWAYS

  • Evidence-based clinical programming. Operational hospital benchmarks ensured radio-shielded bunker parameters, sterile airlocks, and bed-to-OT ratios matched oncology healthcare standards.
  • Circulation separation mitigates contamination. Segregating high-turnover outpatient routes from sterile surgical cores and emergency bays minimized cross-contamination and nosocomial infection risks.
  • Structural innovation enables restorative landscapes. Elevating the inpatient ward block on large-span cantilever piers preserved the permeable ground plane for healing water gardens.
  • Vertical acuity zoning calms occupant experience. Structuring departments from trauma at grade to surgical suites at mid-level and quiet wards above established an intuitive, restorative spatial gradient.
