PALLIATIVE CANCER HOSPICE

Low-Rise Residential-Scaled Hospice · Courtyard Healing · Biophilic Landscape

  • LocationYelahanka, Bengaluru, Karnataka, India
  • ProgrammeBachelor of Architecture — Major Design Studio
  • Scope110-Bed Inpatient Hospice, Wellness & Rejuvenation Core, Covered Verandahs, 20-Acre Master Plan
  • ToolsAutodesk Revit, SketchUp, AutoCAD, Adobe Photoshop, Lumion, DesignBuilder

A residential-scaled palliative care sanctuary set within a 20-acre landscape.

Designed on behalf of an oncology research initiative, the hospice provides comprehensive physical, psychological, and spiritual support for terminally ill patients and their families. The proposal replaces institutional hospital corridors with low-rise, pitched-roof pavilions gathered around mature trees and therapeutic courtyards.

01. Site Context & Master Plan

Landscape-first site planning. Built pavilions occupy a compact footprint along the site edge, leaving the vast majority of the 20-acre property as preserved open meadow and native woodland. A circular forested approach ring buffers vehicle arrivals before reaching the central drop-off.

02. Design Concept & Transition Spaces

Four core ideas shape the architecture: humanizing healthcare through domestic residential scale, framing expansive courtyard vistas from every patient room, elevating verandahs into the tree canopy, and integrating native botanical species as living environmental buffers.

03. Native Landscaping & Microclimate

Six indigenous flowering and shade tree species were chosen to minimize irrigation demands while providing continuous seasonal color and microclimatic cooling across patient outdoor areas.

04. Pavilions, Courtyards & Garden Lounges

Covered verandahs connect all patient rooms, communal activity rooms, and meditation lounges. A circular open garden pavilion allows an existing mature tree to grow directly through its core, offering sheltered outdoor seating within the grove.

05. Floor Plans & Sectional Hierarchy

The building section maintains a low, pitched-roof datum that sits below the mature tree canopy, reinforcing a non-institutional domestic scale for long-term patient recovery.

06. Academic & Design Takeaways

  • 1. Domestic Scaling for Healthcare Approaching a 110-bed clinical hospice through residential architectural language transformed patient care from institutional confinement into a peaceful retreat.
  • 2. Universal Verandah Circulation Continuous perimeter covered walkways enabled mobility-impaired patients to move freely between rooms, courtyards, and communal spaces in all weather conditions.
  • 3. Living Botanical Integration Preserving existing mature trees directly within the floor plates and circulation nodes made landscape an active structural component of healing.
  • 4. Sustainable Regional Planting Selecting native climate-adapted species reduced facility maintenance while delivering restorative seasonal color shifts across the grounds.
